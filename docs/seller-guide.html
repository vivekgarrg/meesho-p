Chapter 1

Start here

Fifteen minutes, once. After this the app runs on the exports you already download from the marketplace.

1

Sign in

Open the site and press Sign in. Your username and password come from whoever set up the account. Change the password from the menu under your name.

2

Pick the right business

If you run more than one shop, the selector at the top left switches between them. Every number on every screen belongs to the business shown there. Check it before you read anything.

3

Upload your first two files

Go to Upload Data. Two exports do most of the work:

  • The Meesho payment file — SP_ORDER_ADS_REFERRAL_PAYMENT_FILE_*.xlsx, or the .zip Meesho gives you, unopened. Don't rename it.
  • Your orders export — Orders*.csv.

Re-uploading the same file is safe. Rows are matched, not duplicated.

4

Tell it what your stock costs

Open SKU Pricing and enter your purchase price for each product. Until you do, the app can show what the marketplace paid you, but not what you made. This is the single most valuable thing you will enter.

Nothing is guessed. Every figure traces back to a file you uploaded or a price you typed. If a number looks wrong, the cause is almost always a missing purchase price or a sheet that hasn't been uploaded yet.

Chapter 2

Parent SKUs and variants

The one idea worth understanding properly. Get this right and pricing, profit and stock all fall into place on their own.

A parent is the product. A child SKU is a version of it you actually list — a size, a colour, a combo pack. You buy the product once at one cost, but you may sell it under six different SKU codes.

Example

Parent: Brass Pooja Thali 7 inch — costs you ₹185
Children: thali-7-plain-001, thali-7-engraved-002, combo-thali-diya-003

Link a child to its parent and it inherits the parent's purchase price, tax rate and packaging cost automatically. Change the parent's price once and every variant follows. You never type the same cost six times, and the six can never drift apart.

Linking, three ways

Suggestions Open a parent in SKU Pricing and press ✨ Suggestions. It reads the parent's name and the variants already under it, then proposes unlinked SKUs that look like they belong — with a match score. One click to link.
Link Center The panel on the left of SKU Pricing lists everything unlinked. Pick a target parent, tick several SKUs, link them in one go — or drag a SKU onto any parent card.
From Labels While looking at shipped labels, any SKU without a parent shows link to parent right there. Useful for catching new variants the day they first ship.

SKUs that will never have a parent

Some SKUs genuinely stand alone — a one-off, a sample, a line you have discontinued. Press the 🚫 button beside it in the Link Center. It disappears from the unlinked pile and stops being suggested, so that pile keeps meaning “still needs attention” instead of slowly filling with things you have already decided about.

It is not deletion. Hidden SKUs keep their pricing and their history. Show N hidden SKUs in the Link Center brings them back at any time.

Chapter 3

The daily routine

Four habits. None of them takes long, and each one catches money that otherwise leaks quietly.

1

Before dispatch — scan the parcels

Order Scan. Point the camera at each parcel as it is packed. The app checks it against the marketplace's own status and stops anything cancelled, already shipped, or scanned twice. A parcel that shouldn't have left the building is the most expensive mistake on this list.

2

When returns arrive — open the claim window

Returns & Claims. Every return that comes back starts a 7-day window in which you can claim for a wrong or damaged item. Scan the packet as evidence and the countdown is on screen. Miss the window and the money is simply gone.

3

Weekly — upload the new payment sheet

Upload Data. Each new payment file fills in what actually settled. Profit, GST and the wallet all firm up as sheets arrive; a recent day legitimately starts out looking incomplete.

4

Weekly — read the Overview

Net profit, what returns cost you, what you owe in GST, and which SKUs are losing money. Ten minutes here is worth more than a day of spreadsheets.

Chapter 4

Reading the money

Where the profit figure comes from, and the two settings that are yours to decide rather than ours to assume.

How profit is worked out

The marketplace has already taken its commission, shipping, TCS and TDS out before it pays you. So the settlement figure is already net of all that — the app does not subtract those a second time. From it, we take off what the goods cost you:

Settlement received − (purchase cost + GST on purchase + packaging) = your profit

Then ad spend, referral income, compensation and — if you switch it on — transport are applied to the bottom line.

Setting 1 · Which orders carry a packaging cost

A box is used the moment an order is packed. Whether a returned or RTO order should still carry that cost is a judgement about your business, not a fact in the data — some sellers write the wasted box off against the return, others only count packaging on orders that actually sold.

On Overview → Packaging Cost, press Change and tick the outcomes you want charged: Delivered, Returned, RTO, Exchange, Claim. Profit updates immediately, everywhere.

Setting 2 · Whether your prices include GST

On Overview → GST Position you'll see what you collected on sales, less what you already paid on the stock that sold. The difference is what you actually hand over.

Get this one right. Marketplace prices normally include GST. On a ₹340 sale at 5%, the tax inside it is ₹16.19 — not ₹17.00 added on top. The checkbox “My sale prices already include GST” should stay ticked unless you genuinely price exclusive of tax. Unticking it overstates your GST bill.

Chapter 5

Paying your team for what they finished

Hand out piecework, review it, and let the wallet keep the ledger. Nobody has to remember who did what.

Setting up, once

  • Open Team Tasks → Rates per platform and set what a listing is worth and what a claim is worth, per marketplace.
  • Pay one person differently if you want — per-worker rates override the standard one.
  • A rate applies to work created from then on. Work already done keeps the rate it had, so nothing is ever silently re-priced.

The listing loop

1

You create the task

Pick the people, paste the photo folder link, and set the starting SKU name (for example BRS-). Add the prices every SKU on the job should use.

2

They generate and send

Your worker presses Generate — the next id in the series appears (BRS-001, BRS-002…) — then Send for approval. Duplicates are refused on the spot.

3

You approve

Approve & pay on each SKU, or Approve all for the batch. Rejecting asks for a reason, which the worker sees so they can fix and resend.

4

The money records itself

Approving credits the worker's wallet and puts the SKU into your catalogue. Mark paid settles the balance; every adjustment needs a reason, so the balance never moves without an explanation.

Approving is what makes a SKU real. A SKU joins your pricing catalogue only when you sign it off — nothing reaches your books without your approval.

Return claim tasks

The same idea for claims: share the video link and the sub-order number, the worker compresses the video under 22 MB and raises the claim, then sends the ticket reference for approval. A bonus can follow automatically if the marketplace approves the claim.

Chapter 6

When something looks wrong

In order of how often it turns out to be the answer.

“Profit looks far too high.”

Purchase prices are missing. Open SKU Pricing and press Show Unpriced — those SKUs are being counted as pure income because nothing tells the app what they cost you.

“A recent day shows almost nothing.”

Correct, and it will fill in. An order only counts once a payment sheet covering it has been uploaded. Recent days firm up over the following weeks.

“The numbers changed since yesterday and I changed nothing.”

A newer payment sheet arrived, or someone edited a purchase price. Both re-work history on purpose, so today's view is always the best available truth rather than a stale snapshot.

“This screen is empty.”

Check the period selector at the top and the business selector at the top left. An empty screen is nearly always the right screen pointed at the wrong month or the wrong shop.

“A SKU keeps appearing as unlinked.”

Either link it to a parent, or press 🚫 to say it will never have one. See Chapter 2.

“My GST bill looks larger than I expected.”

Check the “My sale prices already include GST” box on Overview. Unticked, it adds tax on top of a price that already contained it. See Chapter 4.
